              appearance

              This client is themeable by you, in the spirit of a personal page rather than a colour picker — a theme may restyle anything in this window, layout and hiding included.

              Not built yet, and the order is deliberate. A theme can hide the control that would undo it, so reset has to work without the theme's cooperation before themes exist at all: a native menu item, and a launch flag that applies no theme. Two doors, because they survive different failures.

              Themes are CSS and never HTML, and this window loads no remote origin of any kind — which is what makes a theme you were handed safe to run rather than merely unlikely to misbehave.

              this device

              Anything nothing else needs to know. Empty today, and named so the first local preference has somewhere obvious to land instead of being filed under the network's business.

              identity

              Filed here rather than beside appearance precisely because it feels local and is not: a display name is a claim in the governance log, unique in this network, and never released — it stays bound to you even after you claim another, so nobody inherits it along with what you wrote under it.

              Shown beside enough of your identity to tell two similar names apart, which is an obligation rather than a flourish: uniqueness is decided on a key that deliberately does not fold lookalikes.

              network

              Every member sees this one, and it travels with the network rather than being retyped per installation. It is not an identifier. Names are not unique and nothing makes them so — the network id below is what you actually joined, and is the only thing that should ever be trusted to say which network this is.

              limits

              Validity rules, not suggestions: a record past one of these is refused by every reader, so they are the network's and not yours. They also spend other members' disks and bandwidth, which is why they sit with whoever is accountable for the network rather than with whoever is uploading.

                what this network keeps

                Retention is not deletion, and refuses nothing. Past its window, content stops being re-wrapped and goes dark to anyone who did not already hold it — but a member who already fetched it keeps it, permanently, and no rotation takes that back. Content allowed to go dark cannot be brought back, which is why both windows ship as forever.

                  relay

                  …

                    Look at the relay's own log first. If it shows no connection, nothing reached it and the address or the port is wrong. If it shows one, it replied announcing nothing.

                    Announcing nothing is a relay behind a proxy with no public address set: it hands back its private container address, grants the reservation anyway, and keeps reporting healthy. Its /health shows what it announces.

                    set the relay

                    A relay needs an identity of its own. Generate one, paste it into the host as RELAY_PHRASE, and keep a copy — it is the only way to redeploy the same relay rather than a new one.

                    Shown once and stored nowhere. Anyone holding it can answer as this relay. It is not your identity — yours never leaves this machine and is never displayed.

                    A relay also needs this network's id before it will start — it is RELAY_NETWORK in DI-Relay's configuration.

                    permissions

                    Permissions are held by roles, never by people. There is no per-person grant anywhere in this protocol, so giving one person access means making a role, granting it what they need, and putting only them in it. That is three steps rather than one, and it is the shape of the thing rather than a detour around it.

                    roles

                      A role cannot be deleted — nothing in the governance log expresses removal, so what a role holds can be emptied and its members taken out, and the name stays in replayed history.
